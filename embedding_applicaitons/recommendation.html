1. What is a recommendation system?

A program that looks at items (videos, movies, products, songs) and people and predicts what each person will like next. It turns an overwhelming catalogue into a short personal list.

Why do companies care? Netflix has thousands of titles. If you cannot find something in a minute, you leave. Good recommendations mean more watching, buying and retention.

2. The two main types

Content-based
"You liked this item, so here are items that look similar." Uses the features of the items (genre, actors, text description) and your own history.
Collaborative filtering
"People who behave like you liked this." Ignores what items are; it only uses who liked what. If you and Asha liked the same 10 movies and she loved an 11th, you probably will too. It works well with lots of users, but struggles with brand-new users and items (the cold start problem).

Real products usually combine them (hybrid). In this lesson we go deep on content-based because it builds directly on the vectors and cosine similarity from the semantic search lesson.

3. Content-based recommendation, step by step

Step 1. Describe every item as a vector

Turn each movie into a list of numbers: how much Action, Sci-Fi, Comedy, Romance, Drama, Animation it has (0 to 1). We assigned these by hand for our 11-movie catalogue.

Where do the vectors come from in real life?
  • Tags and genres (like here): simple, readable.
  • Text embeddings: feed the title, description or reviews to an embedding model such as all-MiniLM-L6-v2 and get 384 numbers per item. This is exactly the semantic search lesson, and it captures subtle similarity ("space survival" is close to "lost on Mars") with no manual tagging.
  • Images and audio can be embedded too (movie posters, songs).

Step 2. Measure item similarity with cosine

Same formula as semantic search: cos(A,B) = A·B / (|A| |B|). Close to 1 means the same flavour.


Worked example: The Matrix vs Inception

Step 3. Build the user profile

We have vectors for movies. What is the vector for you? Simple and powerful trick: the average of the vectors of everything you liked.

The profile lives in the same space as the movies, so we can compare you directly with any movie.

Step 4. Score the unseen items and rank

for each movie you have NOT watched: score = cosine(user_profile, movie_vector) recommend the top-N by score

Strengths and weaknesses of content-based

Strengths
  • Works for a brand-new item as soon as it has features.
  • Needs only one user's history, not millions of users.
  • Explainable: "because you like Sci-Fi".
Weaknesses
  • Filter bubble: only similar things; you rarely discover a surprise. (Like only sci-fi and you will never see a comedy. Try it above.)
  • Needs good features; bad tags give bad results.
  • Cold start for users: a new user with no likes has an empty profile.
